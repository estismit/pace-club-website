Why strength matters more after 35

PACE Club · 4 min read

Many women think of strength training as something for athletes or young gym-goers. In reality, it becomes more important with every birthday.

What changes as we get older

From around our thirties, the body slowly starts to lose muscle if we don't actively use it. Researchers estimate this loss at roughly 3 to 8% per decade after the age of 30, and it tends to speed up later in life.

For women, the years around menopause add another layer. As oestrogen levels fall, bone density declines faster. Over time, weaker muscles and bones can make everyday things like climbing stairs, carrying shopping or getting up from the floor feel harder than they should.

What strength training does

The good news is that muscle responds to training at any age. Regular strength work helps you:

  • Keep and build muscle, so everyday tasks stay easy.
  • Support your bones, because bones respond to the load placed on them.
  • Improve balance and stability, which helps you feel steady on your feet.
  • Feel more confident in your body, knowing what it can do.

The World Health Organization recommends that adults do muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days a week, alongside regular movement.

How to start

You don't need heavy weights or hours in a gym. Start with two sessions a week that work your whole body: legs, hips, back, arms and core. Focus on good form first, then gradually make exercises a little harder as you get stronger.

Having a coach makes a real difference here. Someone who watches your technique and adapts exercises to your body helps you progress safely, especially if you have old injuries or haven't trained in a while.

This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or injury, check with your healthcare provider before starting a new exercise programme.
