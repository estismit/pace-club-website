Getting back into exercise after years away

PACE Club · 4 min read

Kids, work, injuries, life. There are a hundred good reasons why exercise falls away. Starting again is less about willpower and more about making it easy to show up.

Start smaller than you think

The most common mistake is doing too much too soon. Your enthusiasm is ready, but your muscles and joints need time to adapt. Two or three shorter sessions a week is a wonderful start. You can always add more later.

Choose consistency over intensity

A moderate session you repeat every week does far more than one exhausting session that leaves you too sore to come back. Aim to finish feeling like you could have done a little more.

Know what normal feels like

Some muscle stiffness a day or two after a new workout is normal and usually settles within a few days. Sharp pain, or pain in a joint, is different. Stop that exercise and mention it to your coach so it can be adapted.

Tell someone about old injuries

An old knee, back or shoulder niggle doesn't mean you can't train. It means the exercises should fit your body. A good coach will ask about this before you start and adjust movements for you.

Put it in your diary

  • Book your sessions like any other appointment.
  • Pick times you can keep, not ideal times.
  • Train with others, so someone notices when you're missing.

Most of all, be kind to yourself. Every session counts, and the hardest one is usually the first.

This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition or injury, check with your healthcare provider before starting a new exercise programme.
